Vocabulary: Synonyms, Antonyms and Idioms

Detailed concept notes
Synonyms and antonyms

A synonym is a word with a similar meaning; an antonym expresses an opposite meaning. A word's exact sense should be checked in its sentence.

Idioms

An idiom is an established expression whose meaning is not always predictable from its individual words. Learn idioms with a short example sentence.

One-word substitution

This tests the ability to express a phrase using one precise word. Pay attention to the exact definition and grammatical role.

Contextual vocabulary

Use surrounding words to identify tone and meaning. Avoid selecting an option merely because it is a familiar synonym.

Important formulas and quick reference
  • Abundant = plentiful.
  • Reluctant = unwilling.
  • Concise = brief but complete.
  • Optimistic = hopeful.
Worked example

Sentence: 'The instructions were concise and easy to follow.'

  1. Concise describes the instructions.
  2. The context suggests clarity without unnecessary length.
  3. Meaning: brief but complete.

Attempt the questions before revealing their solutions.

  1. A. Scarce
  2. B. Plentiful
  3. C. Empty
  4. D. Weak

Abundant means plentiful.

Question 2: Antonym of 'Expand'?

  1. A. Extend
  2. B. Enlarge
  3. C. Contract
  4. D. Increase
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: Contract

Contract means to become smaller.

Advanced

Question 3: 'To break the ice' means:

  1. A. To damage something
  2. B. To begin a friendly conversation
  3. C. To end a meeting
  4. D. To create confusion
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: To begin a friendly conversation

The idiom refers to easing social interaction.

Exam

Question 4: A person who speaks many languages is a:

  1. A. Linguist
  2. B. Polyglot
  3. C. Orator
  4. D. Novelist
Reveal answer and explanation

A polyglot knows or uses several languages.

CORE

Grammar: Tenses and Error Detection

Detailed concept notes
Tense

Tense places an action in time. Simple, continuous, perfect and perfect continuous forms communicate different relationships between actions and time.

Subject–verb agreement

Match the verb to the subject. Ignore intervening phrases when identifying the main subject.

Articles

'A' and 'an' are indefinite articles; 'the' is definite. The choice between a and an depends on the initial sound, not simply the first letter.

Prepositions

Prepositions express relationships such as time, place and direction. Many verb-preposition combinations are idiomatic and must be learnt in context.

Important formulas and quick reference
  • Present simple: subject + base verb (with third-person singular agreement).
  • Present continuous: am/is/are + verb-ing.
  • Present perfect: has/have + past participle.
  • Past perfect: had + past participle.
Worked example

Correct: 'She has completed the assignment.'

  1. Subject is she.
  2. Present perfect uses has + past participle.
  3. Completed is the past participle.
Topic-wise practice

Attempt the questions before revealing their solutions.

Beginner

Question 1: He ___ to the library every day.

  1. A. go
  2. B. goes
  3. C. going
  4. D. gone
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: goes

Third-person singular takes goes.

Intermediate

Question 2: Choose the correct article: '___ honest person'.

  1. A. A
  2. B. An
  3. C. The only
  4. D. No article
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: An

Honest begins with a vowel sound.

Advanced

Question 3: By the time we arrived, the train ___.

  1. A. leaves
  2. B. has left
  3. C. had left
  4. D. will leave
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: had left

The earlier past action uses past perfect.

Exam

Question 4: Identify the correct sentence.

  1. A. Neither of them are ready.
  2. B. Neither of them is ready.
  3. C. Neither of them were ready.
  4. D. Neither of them have ready.
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: Neither of them is ready.

Neither is generally treated as singular in this construction.

Ratio, Proportion and Time–Distance

Detailed concept notes
Ratio

A ratio compares quantities. Convert both quantities into the same unit before simplifying.

Proportion

Two ratios are proportional when their cross-products are equal.

Speed, distance and time

Speed measures distance travelled per unit time. Ensure units are consistent before calculation.

Average speed

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. It is not always the arithmetic mean of two speeds.

Important formulas and quick reference
  • a:b = a/b.
  • a/b = c/d implies ad = bc.
  • Speed = Distance/Time.
  • Distance = Speed × Time.
  • Time = Distance/Speed.
Worked example

A vehicle travels 150 km in 3 hours.

  1. Speed = distance/time.
  2. 150/3 = 50.
  3. Average speed = 50 km/h.
Topic-wise practice

Attempt the questions before revealing their solutions.

Beginner

Question 1: Simplify 12:18.

  1. A. 2:3
  2. B. 3:2
  3. C. 4:9
  4. D. 6:5
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 2:3

Divide both terms by 6.

Intermediate

Question 2: A vehicle travels 120 km in 2 hours. Speed?

  1. A. 40 km/h
  2. B. 50 km/h
  3. C. 60 km/h
  4. D. 80 km/h
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 60 km/h

120/2 = 60 km/h.

Advanced

Question 3: Divide 72 in ratio 5:7. Larger share?

  1. A. 30
  2. B. 35
  3. C. 42
  4. D. 48
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 42

Twelve parts; one part is 6; larger share is 7 × 6.

Exam

Question 4: If speed doubles for the same distance, travel time becomes:

  1. A. Double
  2. B. Half
  3. C. Four times
  4. D. Unchanged
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: Half

Time is inversely proportional to speed for fixed distance.

CORE

Logical Reasoning: Series and Coding

Detailed concept notes
Number series

Check consecutive differences first. If they are not constant, inspect ratios, second differences, squares, cubes or alternating patterns.

Letter series

Map letters to positions in the alphabet. Look for consistent increments or alternating rules.

Coding and decoding

Compare original and coded forms to identify shifts, reversals, substitutions or position-based rules.

Accuracy

Test a proposed rule against every given term. A pattern that explains only part of the series is insufficient.

Important formulas and quick reference
  • Alphabet positions: A = 1 through Z = 26.
  • Square numbers: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25...
  • Cube numbers: 1, 8, 27, 64...
  • Constant difference: a, a+d, a+2d...
Worked example

Find the next term: 3, 6, 12, 24.

  1. Compare consecutive terms.
  2. Each term is multiplied by 2.
  3. Next term = 24 × 2 = 48.
Topic-wise practice

Attempt the questions before revealing their solutions.

Beginner

Question 1: Next term: 5, 10, 15, 20, __.

  1. A. 22
  2. B. 24
  3. C. 25
  4. D. 30
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 25

Add 5 each time.

Intermediate

Question 2: Next term: 1, 4, 9, 16, __.

  1. A. 20
  2. B. 24
  3. C. 25
  4. D. 36
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 25

These are consecutive squares.

Advanced

Question 3: If CAT is coded as DBU by shifting each letter forward by one, DOG becomes:

  1. A. EPH
  2. B. CNG
  3. C. FQI
  4. D. EOG
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: EPH

D→E, O→P, G→H.

Exam

Question 4: Find the missing term: 2, 6, 18, 54, __.

  1. A. 108
  2. B. 126
  3. C. 162
  4. D. 216
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 162

Each term is multiplied by 3.
