Grammar, vocabulary and reading comprehension practice.

FOUNDATION

Subject–Verb Agreement

Detailed concept notes
Core rule

A singular subject generally takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb. The verb agrees with the actual subject, not a nearby noun in a modifying phrase.

Indefinite pronouns

Words such as everyone, somebody and each are generally treated as singular. Words such as many and several are plural.

Either/or and neither/nor

When subjects are joined by either/or or neither/nor, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject.

Collective nouns

Collective nouns may be treated as singular when the group acts as one unit. Usage can vary by context and dialect.

Important formulas and quick reference
  • He works; they work.
  • Each of the students is ready.
  • The list of items is on the desk.
  • Neither the teacher nor the students are absent.
Worked example

Choose the correct form: 'The quality of these products ___ excellent.'

  1. Find the subject: quality.
  2. 'Of these products' is a modifying phrase.
  3. Quality is singular, so use is.
Topic-wise practice

Attempt the questions before revealing their solutions.

Beginner

Question 1: Each of the players ___ ready.

  1. A. are
  2. B. is
  3. C. were
  4. D. have
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: is

Each is singular.

Intermediate

Question 2: The bouquet of roses ___ beautiful.

  1. A. are
  2. B. were
  3. C. is
  4. D. have
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: is

The subject is bouquet, not roses.

Advanced

Question 3: Neither the manager nor the employees ___ available.

  1. A. is
  2. B. was
  3. C. are
  4. D. has
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: are

The nearer subject employees is plural.

Exam

Question 4: Choose the correct sentence.

  1. A. The news are encouraging.
  2. B. The news is encouraging.
  3. C. The news were encouraging.
  4. D. The news have encouraging.
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: The news is encouraging.

News is grammatically singular.

PRACTICE

Vocabulary and Reading Comprehension

Detailed concept notes
Vocabulary in context

Synonyms have similar meanings, while antonyms have opposite meanings. The correct choice depends on context and tone.

Roots and word families

Recognising common prefixes, suffixes and roots can help infer unfamiliar words. For example, 'bene' is associated with good or well.

Comprehension

Read for the central idea, supporting details, tone and inference. An inference must be supported by the passage rather than outside assumptions.

Sentence completion

Use grammar and meaning together. Eliminate options that break the sentence's logic or grammatical structure.

Important formulas and quick reference
  • Synonym: similar meaning.
  • Antonym: opposite meaning.
  • Inference: conclusion supported by evidence.
  • Main idea: central message of a passage.
Worked example

Passage: 'Regular exercise improves stamina and supports general well-being. Consistency is more useful than occasional intense activity.'

  1. Identify the repeated idea: regularity.
  2. Find the contrast: consistency versus occasional intensity.
  3. Main idea: sustainable habits support well-being.
Topic-wise practice

Attempt the questions before revealing their solutions.

Beginner

Question 1: Choose the synonym of 'Abundant'.

  1. A. Scarce
  2. B. Plentiful
  3. C. Empty
  4. D. Rare
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: Plentiful

Abundant means existing in large quantities.

Intermediate

Question 2: Choose the antonym of 'Reluctant'.

  1. A. Unwilling
  2. B. Hesitant
  3. C. Eager
  4. D. Doubtful
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: Eager

Reluctant means unwilling or hesitant.

Advanced

Question 3: A conclusion drawn from clues in a passage is called:

  1. A. A title
  2. B. An inference
  3. C. A quotation
  4. D. A heading
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: An inference

Inference uses evidence to reach a supported conclusion.

Exam

Question 4: Choose the correctly spelt word.

  1. A. Accomodation
  2. B. Acommodation
  3. C. Accommodation
  4. D. Acomodation
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: Accommodation

Accommodation has double c and double m.

Percentages, Profit and Loss

Detailed concept notes
Percentage

Percentage converts a ratio into a value out of 100. Always identify the base value before calculating a percentage change.

Cost price and selling price

Profit occurs when selling price exceeds cost price. Loss occurs when selling price is below cost price.

Discount

Discount is calculated on the marked price. The selling price is the marked price after discount.

Important formulas and quick reference
  • Profit = SP - CP.
  • Loss = CP - SP.
  • Profit% = Profit/CP × 100.
  • Loss% = Loss/CP × 100.
  • Discount = MP - SP.
Worked example

An item costs ₹500 and sells for ₹600.

  1. Profit = 600 - 500 = ₹100.
  2. Profit percentage = 100/500 × 100.
  3. Profit = 20%.
Topic-wise practice

Attempt the questions before revealing their solutions.

Beginner

Question 1: 10% of 450 is:

  1. A. 35
  2. B. 40
  3. C. 45
  4. D. 50
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 45

450 × 10/100 = 45.

Intermediate

Question 2: CP ₹200, SP ₹240. Profit percentage?

  1. A. 10%
  2. B. 15%
  3. C. 20%
  4. D. 25%
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: 20%

Profit = 40; 40/200 × 100 = 20%.

Advanced

Question 3: An item marked ₹800 has a 15% discount. Selling price?

  1. A. ₹620
  2. B. ₹660
  3. C. ₹680
  4. D. ₹700
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: ₹680

Discount = 120; selling price = 800 - 120.

Exam

Question 4: A 20% loss occurs on CP ₹750. Find SP.

  1. A. ₹550
  2. B. ₹575
  3. C. ₹600
  4. D. ₹625
Reveal answer and explanation
Answer: ₹600

SP = 80% of 750 = ₹600.
